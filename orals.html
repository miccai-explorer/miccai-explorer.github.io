Orals & Spotlights, 2021-2025

What happens to a paper after it is accepted

Since 2021, MICCAI has published peer reviews for every accepted paper. What has not been done before is joining that to the list of accepted papers by presentation type (oral, spotlight, or poster-only). Doing so enables us to answer interesting questions about what in the reviews, if anything, separates a talk from a poster: review scores, reviewer confidence, early acceptance, and subject area. 377 talks (orals and spotlights combined) were matched to 3,717 accepted papers across 5 years, which is 10.1% of everything accepted.

The Program

How many papers get a talk, and in which type?

The share of accepted papers that receive a podium presentation slot has stayed roughly consistent. That holds even though spotlight talks started only in 2024, and even though the conference has grown in size from 531 to 1027 papers.

Do Talks Get Better Reviews?

Mean review score by presentation type

The bars show each type's mean score as a percentage of that year's own scale. They are drawn that way because the review score scale has changed over the years, and the whiskers show 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Hover over each bar for more information, including standard deviation and group sizes.

Effect sizes

Cliff's δ captures how much more often papers from one group outscore papers from another. It is well suited for ordinal review scores, and stays comparable across years whose scales differ. In all years, oral papers outscored poster-only papers (δ from +0.28 to +0.74, every interval clear of zero). The gap is much smaller in 2025 (small) than in the years before it. The shaded bands in the plot mark the conventional thresholds for negligible (inner band; |δ| < 0.147) and small (outer band; |δ| < 0.33) effects. And in cases where the bars cross a shaded band, the difference is too small to matter.

Does Early Acceptance Matter?

Early-accepted papers are far more likely to get a talk

Early-accepted papers are heavily over-represented among orals in every year. Early accepts are decided before the rebuttal round, based only on the first round of reviews, so this may well be the reason. The bars below show the share of early-accepted papers within each presentation type, and whiskers show 95% Wilson intervals. Hover over the bars for more information.

Is the score gap just early acceptance in disguise?

No: orals score higher than posters among early-accepted and not-early-accepted papers alike. Comparing the two presentation types within each acceptance group shows that the gap between them persists even after controlling for early acceptance, though it narrows.

Does Reviewer Confidence Matter?

Confidence does not separate the types

Reviewers rate their own confidence on a four-level scale. Orals and posters receive very similar confidence ratings (average of about 3.3 every year), and which type gets (slightly) higher confidence ratings varies by year. So, reviewer confidence tells us nothing about why some papers are chosen for talks. An interesting deviation is the higher-than-expected confidence for spotlight papers in 2024, but this is only 18 papers, so it is hard to draw any concrete conclusions.

Notes & Limitations

  • Presentation types are read from the official MICCAI program books, and review data is scraped from papers.miccai.org. The two are joined on paper title within the same year. Invited talks, panel discussions, and CLINICCAI are excluded.
  • Only accepted papers' reviews are published, so all the analysis only covers the accepted papers. We have no information on what separates an accepted paper from a rejected one.
  • The spotlight category is new (only since 2024) and the number of papers is small (54 papers across 2 years), so their confidence intervals are wide.
