What happens to a paper after it is accepted

Since 2021, MICCAI has published peer reviews for every accepted paper, but this information has not been analyzed before by "joining" it with the list of accepted papers by presentation type (oral, spotlight, or poster-only). Doing so enables us to answer interesting questions about what in the reviews, if anything, separates a talk from a poster: review scores, reviewer confidence, early acceptance, and subject area. {{ orals.total_selected | format_int }} talks (orals and spotlights combined) were matched to {{ orals.total_papers | format_int }} accepted papers across {{ orals.years | length }} years, which is {{ orals.pct_selected }}% of everything accepted.

Do Talks Get Better Reviews?

Mean review score by presentation type

Since the review score scale has changed over the years, the bar heights here show the mean score as a percentage of each year's own scale, and the whiskers show 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Hover over each bar for more information, including standard deviation and group sizes.

Effect sizes

Cliff's δ captures how much more often papers from one group outscore papers from another. It is well suited for ordinal review scores, and stays comparable across years whose scales differ. In all years, oral papers outscored poster-only papers (δ from {{ "%+.2f" | format(orals.delta_min) }} to {{ "%+.2f" | format(orals.delta_max) }}, every interval clear of zero). The gap is much smaller in {{ orals.per_year[-1].year }} ({{ orals.per_year[-1].magnitude }}) than in the years before it. The shaded bands in the plot mark the conventional thresholds for negligible (inner band; |δ| < 0.147) and small (outer band; |δ| < 0.33) effects. And in cases where the bars cross a shaded band, the difference is too small to matter.

Does Early Acceptance Matter?

Early-accepted papers are far more likely to get a talk

Early accepts are decided before the rebuttal round, based only on the first round of reviews. Perhaps consequently, early-accepted papers are heavily over-represented among orals in all years. The bars below show the share of early-accepted papers within each presentation type, and whiskers show 95% Wilson intervals. Hover over the bars for more information.

Is the score gap just early acceptance in disguise?

Comparing the scores of oral and poster papers within each acceptance group (early-accepted and not-early-accepted) shows that the gap between the two presentation types persists even after controlling for early acceptance: orals score higher than posters in both groups.

Does Reviewer Confidence Matter?

Confidence does not separate the types

Reviewers rate their own confidence on a four-level scale. Orals and posters receive very similar confidence ratings (average of about 3.3 every year), and which type gets (slightly) higher confidence ratings varies by year. So, reviewer confidence tells us nothing about why some papers are chosen for talks. An interesting deviation is the higher-than-expected confidence for spotlight papers in 2024, but this is only 18 papers, so it is hard to draw any concrete conclusions.

Notes & Limitations

  • Presentation types are read from the official MICCAI program books, and review data is scraped from papers.miccai.org. The two are joined on paper title within the same year. Invited talks, panel discussions, and CLINICCAI are excluded.
  • Only accepted papers' reviews are published, so all the analysis only covers the accepted papers. We have no information on what separates an accepted paper from a rejected one.
  • The spotlight category is new (only since 2024) and the number of papers is small ({{ orals.total_spotlight }} papers across {{ orals.spotlight_years | length }} years), so their confidence intervals are wide.
